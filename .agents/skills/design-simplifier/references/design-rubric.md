# Design review rubric

Use this rubric to form a judgment, not to manufacture a numerical score. Compare the current and proposed experience for the same user job.

## Review order

1. **Utility** — Does the user reach the intended outcome? Is the outcome the right kind of outcome for the product boundary?
2. **Usability** — Can the user understand what is happening and operate the common path with few decisions and little navigation?
3. **Coherence** — Does the experience use the same scope, language, hierarchy, and primitives as the rest of OpenPresence?
4. **Trust and recovery** — Are state, authority, pending work, failure, cancellation, undo, and verified outcome distinguishable and recoverable?
5. **Desirability** — Does the experience feel calm, considered, alive, and worth returning to?
6. **Delight** — Do details reinforce feedback, progress, trust, or accomplishment without obscuring the task?

## Complexity baseline

For the observed journey, capture comparative indicators:

- steps to first useful outcome;
- decisions and required inputs;
- concepts/nouns the user must understand;
- destinations, modes, modals, and context switches;
- persistent controls and duplicated functionality;
- documentation or explanatory copy required;
- irreversible moments and recovery options.

These indicators diagnose change; they are not universal targets. A longer flow may be correct when it protects privacy, authority, safety, or a meaningful user decision.

## Evidence bar

Strong evidence is a concrete journey observation, user research signal, persisted state, accessibility finding, or relevant precedent that demonstrates material friction. A screenshot alone, personal preference, implementation convenience, or “this could look different” is not enough.

## Change threshold

A recommendation needs at least one material reason: critical-journey friction; duplicate/overlapping concept; unnecessary step/decision; unclear primary action; inconsistent mental model or terminology; unnecessary surface/navigation; poor default; missing feedback/recovery; meaningful accessibility/usability issue; or a strong, context-fit precedent for a simpler solution.

Prioritize structural simplification, critical journeys, coherence, trust/recovery, then polish. Prefer one or two high-leverage changes over a large backlog.
